Capeltic Go-to-Market Strategy

Marketing Strategy · Santa Clara University · Fall 2025 · Team of five

Developed a research-informed customer acquisition and growth strategy for Capeltic, an Indigenous-owned coffee cooperative seeking to expand into US Jesuit universities. Focused on customer segmentation, product storytelling, institutional buyer targeting, customer journey design, KPI development, and execution planning.

What was the question?

How could an Indigenous-owned coffee cooperative turn its mission-driven value proposition into a scalable customer acquisition strategy across US Jesuit universities?

Capeltic had an authentic brand story, premium single-origin coffee, and early traction at Santa Clara University. However, expanding beyond its existing presence required overcoming limited US brand awareness, complex institutional procurement processes, and competition from established coffee suppliers.

Our five-person team developed a go-to-market strategy to support Capeltic’s expansion through Jesuit university and parish networks.

My focus was translating the market opportunity into a clear understanding of who to target, how to reach them, what would motivate adoption, and how to measure success.

What did I own?

I led seven components of the proposed growth strategy:

  • Customer segmentation: Developed three research-informed customer profiles using secondary research to distinguish institutional buyers, student advocates, and recurring consumers.
  • Product and packaging storytelling: Proposed ways to communicate Capeltic’s Indigenous ownership, product quality, and social impact through packaging and customer touchpoints.
  • Institutional prospect identification: Identified prospective university customers and relevant food-service decision-makers to support B2B outreach.
  • Social channel research: Evaluated campus and student-facing social profiles to identify potential awareness and promotional channels.
  • KPI development: Defined measurable acquisition, engagement, retention, and revenue-growth targets.
  • Implementation roadmap: Built a phased execution plan covering the first 30 days through one year.
  • Customer journey: Mapped how different stakeholders could progress from awareness to trial, adoption, and repeat purchasing.
Who were we actually trying to reach?

Using secondary research, I developed three customer segments with different motivations and roles in the purchasing process.

Customer segmentation and purchase influence

Decision-makersApprove suppliers
StudentsGenerate demand
Faculty & staffDrive retention

Institutional decision-makers: The Purpose-Driven Leader. Campus food-service and Ignatian directors who evaluate supplier reliability, institutional mission alignment, ethical sourcing, and sustainability. These stakeholders influence whether Capeltic can secure a campus partnership.

Student advocates: The Campus Changemaker. Students and mission-driven campus organizations motivated by social impact, community participation, and new experiences. These audiences can generate awareness, encourage product trials, and build grassroots demand.

Faculty and staff: The Conscious Caffeine Lover. Everyday coffee consumers who value quality, convenience, and ethical purchasing. Their repeat purchases could help sustain demand after initial campus activation.

The strategic insight: Winning institutional approval and generating consumer demand required different approaches. Capeltic needed a B2B strategy to reach procurement decision-makers alongside a consumer engagement strategy that encouraged trial and repeat purchasing.

How could packaging become part of the product experience?

Capeltic’s Indigenous ownership and end-to-end production story offered meaningful differentiation, but that value needed to be communicated at the point of purchase.

I developed packaging storytelling recommendations that connected the physical product to the people and communities producing it.

The proposed experience included:

  • Origin storytelling: Highlighting the cooperative’s growers, community, and production journey.
  • Mission-driven design: Using culturally relevant artwork and messaging to communicate the brand’s identity.
  • Digital connection: Incorporating QR codes that could connect customers with grower stories and cooperative impact.
  • Practical usability: Providing clear brewing instructions to improve the at-home product experience.

The goal was to move beyond positioning Capeltic as simply a socially responsible purchase and reinforce its value as a high-quality coffee product with a distinctive origin story.

How would we find the right customers?

I identified prospective institutional buyers across Jesuit universities, including food-service directors, culinary leaders, and sustainability-focused procurement stakeholders.

I also researched campus-affiliated social media profiles and potential promotional partners to understand which channels could support student awareness.

This informed a two-part acquisition approach:

Institutional acquisition: Personalized outreach, discovery conversations, product samples, and campus pilots designed to build credibility with university decision-makers.

Consumer activation: Campus tastings, student organization partnerships, social media promotion, and loyalty incentives designed to create awareness and encourage repeat purchases.

Together, these channels would help Capeltic build demand among consumers while pursuing the institutional partnerships needed to support wider distribution.

How could we turn awareness into repeat customers?

I developed a customer journey using the Attention, Interest, Desire, and Action framework, adapting each stage to the needs of institutional buyers, students, and faculty and staff.

Customer journey

01AttentionAwareness
02InterestSampling
03DesirePreference
04ActionAdoption

Attention: Build awareness. Introduce Capeltic through targeted institutional outreach, campus communications, social media, and product storytelling.

Interest: Encourage discovery. Use coffee tastings, sample kits, grower stories, and mission-aligned campus events to help audiences understand the product and its purpose.

Desire: Build preference. Reinforce premium coffee quality, Indigenous ownership, and institutional values alignment to give customers a compelling reason to choose Capeltic over existing alternatives.

Action: Drive adoption and retention. Convert institutional interest into pilot orders and potential supply agreements while encouraging consumer purchases through café availability, campus activations, and loyalty programs.

The customer journey helped connect marketing activities to specific behavioral objectives rather than treating brand awareness as the end goal.

How would we execute the strategy?

I developed a phased implementation roadmap that sequenced foundational research, customer activation, and institutional expansion over a one-year period.

First 30 days: Establish the foundation. Evaluate available marketing resources, develop a budget, and build a targeted list of Jesuit university contacts.

Days 45–60: Prepare for customer activation. Develop new beverage concepts, prepare tasting materials, and launch sampling and promotional activities on campus.

Day 90: Expand engagement. Introduce campus pop-ups, student ambassadors, and participation in university events to reach audiences beyond existing café customers.

Six months: Measure and optimize. Evaluate acquisition and engagement metrics, refine marketing activities, and begin outreach to additional Jesuit universities.

One year: Pursue institutional expansion. Use early campaign learnings and campus demand to support conversations about new university partnerships and potential supply agreements.

This roadmap established a progression from low-cost market activation to broader institutional growth, with performance measurement informing subsequent investment decisions.

What was the outcome?

Our team delivered a comprehensive go-to-market proposal for Capeltic’s potential US university expansion.

My contributions translated secondary customer research into actionable audience segments, identified institutional prospects and promotional channels, developed packaging and customer experience recommendations, and established a measurable execution plan.

The project produced a proposed growth strategy, not a completed commercial rollout. Its value was providing a structured framework for Capeltic to test demand, strengthen customer engagement, and evaluate expansion opportunities across the Jesuit university network.

What I took away: A strong mission creates differentiation, but sustainable growth requires understanding who makes purchasing decisions, what motivates customers, how they experience the product, and which metrics indicate that the strategy is working.

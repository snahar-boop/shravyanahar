How do you drive digital growth with a two-person team?

At WaterScience, I worked across digital marketing, customer discovery, and e-commerce growth, using customer insights and performance data to identify opportunities, improve conversion, and build the business case for new investments.

What was the situation?

WaterScience was a growing consumer products startup with an ambitious digital growth agenda and a lean team. With the CFO and me handling much of the marketing function, resources were limited, and every investment needed to demonstrate value.

The challenge was to understand which customers to prioritize, which channels were performing, and where we could improve the digital customer journey to drive more efficient growth.

What did I own?

I owned full-funnel discovery and lifecycle optimization across 15 marketing campaigns, from customer research and acquisition through conversion and engagement.

My responsibilities spanned three key areas:

Customer discovery and growth strategy

Analyzed customer behavior and web analytics to identify high-potential segments, understand conversion opportunities, and inform campaign and feature priorities.

Digital product and e-commerce optimization

Worked on the Shopify website revamp, Amazon storefront, and digital customer experience, partnering with external engineering and creative agencies to support development and adoption.

Business cases and investment decisions

Built data-backed recommendations for new features, customer segments, and marketing initiatives, securing alignment from the CEO and CMO and supporting expanded investment.

I also launched the WaterWise blog and SEO initiative to strengthen the brand’s organic content and discovery strategy.

How did I use the data?

Rather than treating each marketing channel independently, I used web analytics and campaign performance to evaluate where customers were entering the funnel, where conversion opportunities existed, and which activities justified additional investment.

These insights informed campaign optimization, customer targeting, and recommendations for improving the digital shopping experience.

I also translated the findings into business cases for leadership, connecting customer opportunities with the commercial rationale for investing in them.

Did it work?

Across 15 campaigns, our optimization efforts contributed to a 20% improvement in conversion rates and a doubling of return on ad spend.

Beyond campaign performance, my data-backed business cases helped secure CEO and CMO alignment and additional investment in digital growth opportunities.

The experience gave me direct exposure to how customer research, analytics, product improvements, and commercial priorities intersect in a resource-constrained business.

What did I take away?

Working in a lean startup taught me to think beyond individual marketing campaigns and focus on the entire customer journey.

It reinforced the importance of understanding customer behavior, identifying the highest-impact opportunities, and making a compelling business case before committing resources.

These were also the skills that drew me further toward product management: connecting customer needs with business outcomes and bringing different teams together to deliver on them.

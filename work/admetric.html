Can AdMetric Ethically Increase Their Profit?

Ethics for Managers · Santa Clara University · Fall 2025 · Team of six

We applied an ethics framework to an ad-tech case, and I built the algorithmic section that defined where persuasion becomes manipulation.

What was the question?

In the case, behavioral nudges lifted conversion from 2% to 3.5% but nearly doubled 90-day churn. Is that profit ethical?

What did I own?

I owned the quantitative analysis of AdMetric’s behavioral nudges, modeling how conversion, customer acquisition cost (CAC), churn, and returns affected customer lifetime value (LTV).

I compared baseline and nudge scenarios to evaluate the trade-off between short-term acquisition gains and long-term customer retention. While conversion increased from 2% to 3.5% and CAC decreased from $45 to $30, 90-day churn rose from 18% to 34%.

I then translated these findings into two complementary frameworks:

  • Financial sustainability: Evaluated LTV/CAC over time to assess whether stronger acquisition performance translated into sustainable customer economics.
  • Ethical tolerance limits: Defined measurable conditions around churn, conversion, returns, and LTV/CAC to identify when a nudge should be disabled rather than optimized further.
LTV/CAC Analysis: Short-Term Growth vs. Long-Term Value

I modeled customer economics over 180 days, comparing baseline performance against actual and ideal nudge scenarios. Although nudges improved acquisition efficiency, the actual LTV/CAC ratio reached only 1.14, below the 1.5 financial sustainability target, highlighting the gap between short-term conversion gains and sustainable customer value.

The key insight was that improving conversion and acquisition efficiency does not automatically mean a product is creating sustainable value for customers or the business.

Beyond the quantitative modeling, I contributed to our team’s evaluation of whether AdMetric should continue using behavioral nudges.

Our team recommended a controlled approach that balanced experimentation and growth with transparency, customer autonomy, and measurable safeguards against manipulation.

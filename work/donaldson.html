What was the opportunity?

With the high-temperature 3D printing market poised for expansion, Donaldson BOFA saw an opportunity to develop a new filtration solution for industrial printers.

The challenge? Conventional carbon filtration struggled at elevated temperatures, while heat affected everything from blowers and sensors to adhesives and external casings.

Did we really need to engineer for every temperature?

As design iterations became increasingly complex and expensive, I questioned whether we needed to support the highest possible operating temperatures.

I analyzed commercially available printers, their maximum operating temperatures, and the thermal requirements of high-performance printing materials.

Based on this research, I proposed capping our product’s operating temperature at 100°C, allowing us to address an estimated 70% of the target market while reducing engineering complexity.

What happens when customers approve a product that isn’t ready?

We sent prototypes to three customers, who responded positively to the first iteration.

However, long-term internal testing revealed that certain accessible surfaces became too hot for safe contact.

Addressing the issue required a combination of casing redesign, additional insulation, and material substitutions before the product could be shipped.

How do you innovate when resources are limited?

As Donaldson BOFA’s first innovative NPD initiative in seven years, the project frequently competed with established revenue-generating products for resources.

I helped keep development moving by coordinating across functions, supporting work outside my formal responsibilities, and leveraging our sourcing network to manage increasing component costs.

Did it actually work?

The product successfully launched one month after my departure from Donaldson BOFA.

While I don’t have post-launch commercial results, the experience reinforced one of my most important product management lessons:

The goal isn’t to build everything technically possible. It’s to build what the market needs, at a cost and complexity that make commercial sense.
